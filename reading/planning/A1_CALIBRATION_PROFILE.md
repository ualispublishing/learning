# Early A1 Calibration Profile

This profile refines the general passage standard for the **first A1 units**. It exists because the first 18-passage audit showed that a fixed 90-word minimum encouraged unnecessary lexical sprawl before the reading path had established enough vocabulary.

## Unit 01 length ramp

These are calibration ranges, not rigid quotas:

| Passage | Primary role | Target words |
|---|---|---:|
| P1 | controlled introduction | 60–85 |
| P2 | near reinforcement | 70–90 |
| P3 | interleaving | 75–100 |
| P4 | transfer | 80–105 |
| P5 | integration | 85–110 |
| P6 | fluency/checkpoint | 90–120 |

Unit 02 may move mostly into the 80–120 range. Later A1 units should gradually converge on the general 90–140 standard, with occasional longer texts only after the lexical/discourse base can support them.

**Reason:** passage length is not CEFR difficulty by itself. At the beginning of A1, lexical control and natural repetition matter more than satisfying an arbitrary minimum word count.

## Lexical load for Unit 01

### Explicit prerequisite exposure band

Unit 01 may draw freely from a carefully reviewed subset of the **rank 1–500 frequency backbone** because these words are also available in the project's flashcard resources.

This is an **exposure prerequisite**, not a claim of mastery. The reader/app may later preteach or verify this subset before a passage. Performance data can override the assumption for an individual learner.

### New material

Per passage:
- P1–P2: preferably **1–2 deliberate retrieval targets** plus at most **1–2 verified pedagogical-support types**;
- P3–P5: usually **1–2 deliberate targets** plus at most **2–3 support types**, with most support already seen earlier in the unit;
- P6: **no deliberately new target and no new support item**.

A repeated inflected form is not a new type when it is morphologically transparent at the current stage.

## What counts as acceptable support

An item outside the explicit prerequisite band must be one of:
1. a deliberate contextual inference/retrieval target;
2. a verified pedagogical-support item recorded under `reading/lexicons/*_a1_support.json`;
3. a transparent grammar/function/morphological form already supported by the curriculum;
4. a proper name or transparent number that does not create lexical burden.

Anything else is an uncontrolled unknown and should normally be rewritten away.

## Context design

Early A1 should prefer **narrow reading**: the same people, places, and concrete situations recur across P1–P3 so new words can be inferred with minimal background load. P4–P5 shift context enough to require retrieval rather than mere recognition. P6 combines only already-supported material.

Natural repetition is desirable; mechanical repetition is not. Reuse a word because the event requires it, not because a frequency counter needs another occurrence.

## Question ramp

P1–P2:
- gist or one explicit detail;
- one reference/sequence item;
- one contextual-vocabulary inference per deliberate target;
- one cloze transfer.

P3–P5:
- retain explicit comprehension;
- increase cause/effect, reference, and contrast;
- cloze should use the target in a **new sentence**, not copy the source sentence.

P6:
- no vocabulary-definition question unless diagnostic;
- prioritize gist, reference, cause, sequence, and one short summary;
- reading speed is valid only if comprehension clears the project gate.

## Approval condition

Unit 01 is not approved merely because its text is grammatical. Each passage must also show:
- natural contemporary language;
- controlled lexical burden under the policy above;
- inferable deliberate targets;
- correct questions and answer key;
- no new lexical material in P6;
- no source-gloss propagation without learner-sense checking.
